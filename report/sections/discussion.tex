\subsection{Trade-offs: accuracy, memory and compute}
\label{sec:trade-offs}

\paragraph{Accuracy and training effort.}
Model~B reaches \FinalBAccuracy\% with \FinalBParams{} parameters.
MobileNetV2 and EfficientNet-B0 reach \FinalMobileAccuracy\% and
\FinalEfficientAccuracy\%, respectively: gains of \FinalMobileGap{} and
\FinalEfficientGap{} percentage points over Model~B. This accuracy advantage
survives the longer custom-model training budget, although its size is much
smaller than the early comparison would suggest. Model~B's selected checkpoint
is epoch \FinalBSelectedEpoch{} within a \FinalBEpochs{}-epoch budget. This
achieved result does not prove an irreducible capacity limit.
Conversely, its lower per-epoch time does
not make it cheaper to train to this result: multiplying the recorded mean
epoch time by the run budget gives \FinalMobileTrainingRatio{} times the
MobileNetV2 epoch-time total and \FinalEfficientTrainingRatio{} times the
EfficientNet-B0 total. These totals exclude historical ImageNet pretraining,
validation, final test evaluation and setup.

\paragraph{Storage versus working memory.}
Model~B occupies \FinalBSize{}~KiB on disk. MobileNetV2 and EfficientNet-B0
require \FinalMobileStorageRatio{} and \FinalEfficientStorageRatio{} times
as many saved-state bytes. A concrete reference is the
\href{https://www.st.com/en/microcontrollers-microprocessors/stm32f407vg.html}{STMicroelectronics
STM32F407VG}, with $1$~MiB flash and $192$~KiB system SRAM, including
$64$~KiB of core-coupled memory (separate backup SRAM is not counted).
Model~B's saved weights are below that flash budget; neither SOTA saved state
is. Firmware and runtime storage must also fit, so this is only a necessary
storage condition, not a deployment demonstration.

SRAM is a stricter obstacle. The generated layer table in
Appendix~\ref{app:layer-tables} gives Model~B's first pointwise output as
$32\times62\times62$. Materialising just that tensor in float32 needs
$32\times62\times62\times4=492{,}032$ bytes, or about $480.5$~KiB, before
other live tensors, operator workspace and the stack. It exceeds both the
named target's SRAM and a hypothetical $256$~KiB SRAM budget even if weights
stay in flash. Thus the unmodified full-tensor implementation has not been
shown to fit this microcontroller. A different memory schedule would need
separate verification; no embedded implementation or peak-RAM measurement
is claimed here.

\paragraph{Arithmetic cost and measured time.}
At native resolution MobileNetV2 uses \FinalMobileMACRatio{} times and
EfficientNet-B0 \FinalEfficientMACRatio{} times Model~B's MACs per image.
Depthwise separable convolution uses a two-step construction: for a spatial
kernel of width $F$, $K$ input channels and $L$ output channels, the regular
cost per location is $F^2KL$, whereas depthwise plus pointwise costs
$F^2K+KL$. Model~B also uses global average
pooling to reduce its dense input. Both SOTA networks already exploit
efficient convolution blocks; Model~B's additional saving comes from its
smaller overall architecture. The profiler counts convolution and linear
MACs, excluding activation functions, normalisation, pooling and memory
traffic. It therefore supports an arithmetic comparison, not a prediction
of latency or energy. In particular, the Tesla~T4 epoch-time ratios are much
smaller than the MAC ratios because training also pays for data loading,
augmentation, backward passes and device-specific kernels.

\paragraph{What pretraining and resolution establish.}
Table~\ref{tab:sota-comparison} separates frozen from fully fine-tuned
backbones at each resolution. Their improvement at fixed $64\times64$
supports adapting the pretrained representation to satellite imagery;
it does not measure the benefit of ImageNet initialisation in isolation.
There is no same-architecture SOTA run trained from random initialisation,
so architecture, capacity and pretraining remain confounded in the comparison
with Model~B. ImageNet-to-EuroSAT domain shift is a plausible reason for the
frozen-feature deficit, rather than an experimentally established mechanism.

The supplementary $224\times224$ runs upsample the same native images;
they add no new sensor detail. Each network's fine-tuned native-resolution
result exceeds its frozen upsampled result, while upsampling after fine-tuning
adds less than one percentage point for substantially more arithmetic.
This shows why the final comparison keeps native input and treats
upsampling separately: a resolution gain must not be credited to pretraining
or presented as free accuracy under an edge compute budget.

\paragraph{Choosing under constraints and limitations.}
A custom model is attractive for a fixed label set and sensor resolution when
storage and arithmetic ceilings dominate. Its weights are learnt directly
from the project's data and its architecture can be controlled without
depending on a supplied pretrained checkpoint. This does not remove the
dataset and software licences. Pretrained networks offer stronger achieved
accuracy here and a practical starting point when development time or
labelled data are limited, especially for domains closer to their source
data. Those general advantages were not isolated by a data-size or
development-time experiment in this study.

Accuracy per KiB describes storage efficiency, but cannot replace an
application's minimum acceptable accuracy or SRAM and latency constraints.
Among the pretrained options, EfficientNet-B0 gains only
\FinalSOTAGap{} percentage points over MobileNetV2, corresponding to
\FinalSOTAExtraCorrect{} extra correct test predictions, with substantially
larger storage and MAC requirements. Without repeated seeds this small gap
does not establish reliable superiority. More broadly, these are single-seed
results on one image split: they do not demonstrate geographic transfer,
robustness to a different sensor, or uncertainty in the model ranking.

\subsection{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

For a strict parameter and storage ceiling, Model~B provides the strongest
resource saving at a measurable accuracy cost, and longer training narrows
that cost without increasing inference resources. Where the larger weight
budget is available and accuracy matters more, fine-tuned MobileNetV2 at
native resolution is the more economical pretrained choice in these runs.
EfficientNet-B0's small observed accuracy gain does not justify its larger
footprint without an application-specific need. None of these choices is a
verified microcontroller deployment: activation memory, target latency and
energy remain unmeasured, even for the sub-100k model.
