\subsection{Trade-offs: accuracy, memory and compute}
\label{sec:trade-offs}

\paragraph{Accuracy and training effort.}
Model~B achieved the accuracy of \FinalBAccuracy\% using \FinalBParams{} parameters.
MobileNetV2 and EfficientNet-B0 reach \FinalMobileAccuracy\% and
\FinalEfficientAccuracy\%, respectively , This means they performed \FinalMobileGap{} and
\FinalEfficientGap{} percentage points better than Model~B. Even after training Model B 
for more epochs, MobileNetV2 and EfficientNet-B0 still performed better. However, the 
difference was much smaller than it appeared in the earlier comparison. Model B's best 
result came from epoch \FinalBSelectedEpoch{}, out of the \FinalBEpochs{} epochs it was
trained for.
However, this does not prove that Model B cannot perform better. It only shows the 
best result achieved in this experiment. More training or different settings 
might improve its performance.
On the other hand, its lower per-epoch time does
not make it cheaper to train to this result: multiplying the recorded mean
epoch time by the run budget gives \FinalMobileTrainingRatio{} times the
MobileNetV2 epoch-time total and \FinalEfficientTrainingRatio{} times the
EfficientNet-B0 total. These estimates exclude previous ImageNet pretraining, 
validation, final testing, and setup time.

\paragraph{Storage versus working memory.}
Model~B takes up \FinalBSize{}~KiB on disk. MobileNetV2 and EfficientNet-B0
require \FinalMobileStorageRatio{} and \FinalEfficientStorageRatio{} times
more storage, respectively. In comparison, the STM32F407VG microcontroller~\cite{st2026stm32f407vg}
has 1 MiB of flash memory and 192 KiB of system SRAM. Flash memory stores 
the model weights, while SRAM is used for temporary data during execution. 
The SRAM includes 64 KiB of core coupled memory. But, the separate backup SRAM 
is not included in this comparison.
Model~B's saved weights are below that flash budget. Neither SOTA saved state
is. Firmware and runtime storage must also fit, so this is only a necessary
storage condition, not a deployment demonstration.

SRAM is a greater challenge. Model~B's first pointwise output as
$32\times62\times62$ is given by the generated layer table in
Appendix~\ref{app:layer-tables}. Materialising just that tensor in float32 needs
$32\times62\times62\times4=492{,}032$ bytes, or about $480.5$~KiB, before
other live tensors, operator workspace and the stack. It exceeds both the
named target's SRAM and a hypothetical $256$~KiB SRAM budget even if weights
stay in flash. therefore the unmodified full-tensor implementation has not been
shown to fit this microcontroller. A different memory schedule would need
separate verification and no embedded implementation or peak-RAM measurement
is claimed here.

\paragraph{Arithmetic cost and measured time.}
In the original image resolution, MobileNetV2 use \FinalMobileMACRatio{} times and
EfficientNet-B0 \FinalEfficientMACRatio{} times Model~B's MACs per image.
Depthwise separable convolution uses a two-step construction: for a spatial
kernel of width $F$, $K$ input channels and $L$ output channels, the regular
cost per location is $F^2KL$, whereas depthwise plus pointwise costs
$F^2K+KL$. Model~B also uses global average
pooling to reduce its dense input. However, MobileNetV2 and EfficientNet-B0 
already use efficient convolution blocks. Therefore, Model B's additional 
reduction in computation mainly comes from its smaller overall architecture.
The profiler measures MACs from convolutional and linear layers. It does not 
include activation functions, normalisation, pooling or memory traffic. 
Therefore, these results allow us to compare the amount of arithmetic 
computation, but they cannot directly predict actual execution time 
or energy consumption.

In particular, the differences in training time measured on the Tesla 
T4 GPU are much smaller than the differences in MAC counts.
Training time also depends on data loading, data augmentation
and framework overhead. In addition to that, GPUs process different types of
operations with different levels of efficiency. These factors may help  
explain the difference, but they were not measured separately in this study.

\paragraph{What pretraining and resolution establish.}
Table~\ref{tab:sota-comparison} separates frozen from fully fine-tuned
backbones at each resolution. Their improvement at fixed $64\times64$
supports adapting the pretrained representation to satellite imagery;
it does not measure the benefit of ImageNet initialisation in isolation.
There is no same-architecture SOTA run trained from random initialisation.
Hence , architecture, capacity and pretraining remain confounded in the comparison
with Model~B. The lower accuracy of frozen models may be due to the difference 
between ImageNet images and EuroSAT satellite images. 

The supplementary $224\times224$ runs upsample the same native images;
they add no new sensor detail. The fine-tuned models at the original 
resolution achieve higher accuracy than the frozen models using enlarged 
images. Further, enlarging the images after fine-tuning improves 
accuracy by less than one percentage point, while also requiring substantially 
more computation. This is showing why the final comparison keeps native input and treats
upsampling separately:it's because a resolution gain must not be credited to pretraining
or presented as free accuracy under an edge compute budget.

\paragraph{Choosing under constraints and limitations.}
A custom model is attractive for a fixed label set and sensor resolution when
storage and arithmetic ceilings dominate. Although the dataset and software 
licences still apply, its weights are learned directly 
from the project's dataset, and its architecture can be designed to meet 
specific requirements without relying on an existing pretrained model.
Pretrained networks offer stronger achieved
accuracy here and a practical starting point when development time or
labelled data are limited, especially for domains closer to their source
data. Those general advantages were not isolated by a data-size or
development-time experiment in this study.

Accuracy per KiB describes storage efficiency, but cannot replace an
application's minimum acceptable accuracy or SRAM and latency constraints.
Among the pretrained options, EfficientNet-B0 gains only
\FinalSOTAGap{} percentage points over MobileNetV2, which corresponds to
\FinalSOTAExtraCorrect{} extra correct test predictions, with substantially
larger storage and MAC requirements. Without repeated seeds this small gap
does not establish reliable superiority. More generally, the results are 
based on a single random seed and one dataset split. Therefore, they do 
not establish whether the models will perform well on images from different 
geographical locations or other sensors. They also do not measure the 
uncertainty in the model rankings.

\subsection{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Model B is the best option when the available parameters and storage are 
very limited. However, this comes with lower accuracy. Training it for 
longer reduces the accuracy difference without increasing the 
resources needed during inference. When more storage is available 
and accuracy is a higher priority, fine-tuned MobileNetV2 at the original 
image resolution is the more resource-efficient pretrained option in these experiments.
EfficientNet-B0's small observed accuracy gain does not justify its larger
footprint without an application-specific need. None of these choices is a
verified microcontroller deployment: activation memory, target latency and
energy remain unmeasured, even for the sub-100k model.
