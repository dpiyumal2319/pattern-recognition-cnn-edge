\subsection{Custom architecture design}
\label{sec:custom-architecture}

Both custom networks accept a $64\times64$ RGB image and produce ten class
logits.  Each feature-extraction
stage uses a valid (unpadded), stride--1 $3\times3$ convolutional
operation, ReLU, and $2\times2$ stride--2 max pooling.  Thus the spatial sequence is
$64\rightarrow62\rightarrow31\rightarrow29\rightarrow14\rightarrow12
\rightarrow6$.  Both networks use channel widths 32, 64, and 128 and hidden
fully connected widths 128 and 64.  This holds the representation widths fixed
so that the resource difference comes from two deliberate substitutions:
standard convolution by depthwise separable convolution, and flattening by
global average pooling (GAP).  Both models use biases in all convolutional
and dense layers, no batch normalisation, and dropout with probability 0.2
immediately before each hidden dense layer during training.  Dropout is
disabled for validation and inference.  The final dense layer returns logits
without a ReLU or softmax.

A standard convolution has
\begin{equation}
  P_{\mathrm{conv}}=(K_hK_wC_{\mathrm{in}}+1)C_{\mathrm{out}},
\end{equation}
where the added one is one bias per output channel.  A depthwise--pointwise
pair instead has
\begin{equation}
  P_{\mathrm{sep}}=(K_hK_w+1)C_{\mathrm{in}}
  +(C_{\mathrm{in}}+1)C_{\mathrm{out}}.
\end{equation}
A dense layer has $(N_{\mathrm{in}}+1)N_{\mathrm{out}}$ parameters.  For one
image, convolution MACs are counted as
$K_hK_wC_{\mathrm{in}}C_{\mathrm{out}}H_{\mathrm{out}}W_{\mathrm{out}}$
and dense MACs as $N_{\mathrm{in}}N_{\mathrm{out}}$; bias additions are not
counted.  Max pooling, ReLU, dropout, flatten, and GAP have no trainable
parameters.  To match the project profiler exactly, pooling and averaging
operations are also outside the reported MAC total.

the first dense layer.  Table~\ref{tab:model-a-hand-derivation} shows that this
flattened head, rather than the convolutions, dominates its parameter count.

\subsubsection{Model B: depthwise separable CNN}

For each Model B stage, the depthwise step applies one $3\times3$ filter to
each input channel and the pointwise $1\times1$ step mixes those channels.
ReLU follows the pointwise step; there is no activation between the
depthwise and pointwise convolutions.  The two rows are kept separate in
Table~\ref{tab:model-b-hand-derivation} to show the cost of each step.
GAP reduces the $128\times6\times6$ tensor to 128 channel means before the
dense stack, avoiding the $4{,}608\rightarrow128$ layer that dominates Model A.

head contains $25{,}418$, placing Model B $63{,}032$ parameters below the
100,000-parameter constraint.  A flattened Model B would retain the
$589{,}952$-parameter first dense layer and would therefore fail the budget;
narrowing all feature channels was rejected because it would also reduce the
feature extractor's representational width.

\subsubsection{Compute saving and profiler cross-check}

Table~\ref{tab:separable-mac-saving} isolates the convolution substitution at
identical input/output shapes.  Across the three stages it reduces convolution
MACs from $29{,}439{,}360$ to $3{,}699{,}980$, a $7.96\times$ reduction.  The
reduction increases as the channel count grows:
for $256\rightarrow256$, the exact per-location costs are
$9\times256\times256=589{,}824$ and
$9\times256+256\times256=67{,}840$, giving an $8.69\times$ reduction.

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering
  \caption{Direct MAC comparison between standard and depthwise separable
  convolution at each common stage shape.}
  \label{tab:separable-mac-saving}
  \begin{tabular}{lrrr}
    \hline
    Stage ($C_{\mathrm{in}}\rightarrow C_{\mathrm{out}}$) & Standard & Separable & Reduction \\
    \hline
    $3\rightarrow32$, $62\times62$ & $3{,}321{,}216$ & $472{,}812$ & $7.02\times$ \\
    $32\rightarrow64$, $29\times29$ & $15{,}501{,}312$ & $1{,}964{,}576$ & $7.89\times$ \\
    $64\rightarrow128$, $12\times12$ & $10{,}616{,}832$ & $1{,}262{,}592$ & $8.41\times$ \\
    \hline
    Convolution total & $29{,}439{,}360$ & $3{,}699{,}980$ & $7.96\times$ \\
    \hline
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Including the different classifier heads, Model B uses $18.72\times$ fewer
trainable parameters (a $94.66\%$ reduction) and $8.06\times$ fewer MACs per
image (an $87.60\%$ reduction) than Model A.  These are arithmetic savings,
not measured inference speedups: memory traffic, kernel support, and the
operations omitted from the MAC count also affect latency.  Finally, the hand-derived totals
were evaluated independently with the forward-hook profiler: the generated
profile values were exactly $692{,}106$ parameters and
$30{,}038{,}016$ MACs for Model A, and $36{,}968$ parameters and $3{,}725{,}196$
MACs for Model B.  The zero discrepancies show that the tables describe the
implemented networks rather than an idealised architecture.
