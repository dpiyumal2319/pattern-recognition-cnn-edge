\subsection{Custom architecture design}
\label{sec:custom-architecture}

Both custom networks take a $64\times64$ RGB image and output ten class
logits.  Each feature-extraction stage uses a valid (unpadded), stride--1
$3\times3$ convolutional operation, ReLU, and $2\times2$ stride--2 max pooling.
The spatial sizes are
$64\rightarrow62\rightarrow31\rightarrow29\rightarrow14\rightarrow12
\rightarrow6$.  Both networks have channel widths 32, 64, and 128 and hidden
fully connected widths 128 and 64.  Model B replaces Model A's standard
convolutions with depthwise separable convolutions and its flattened head
with global average pooling (GAP).

All convolutional and dense layers have biases.  Neither model uses batch
normalisation.  During training, dropout with probability 0.2 is applied
immediately before each hidden dense layer.  It is disabled for validation
and inference.  The final dense layer outputs logits without a ReLU or
softmax.

A standard convolution has
\begin{equation}
  P_{\mathrm{conv}}=(K_hK_wC_{\mathrm{in}}+1)C_{\mathrm{out}},
\end{equation}
where the added one is one bias per output channel.  A depthwise--pointwise
pair instead has
\begin{equation}
  P_{\mathrm{sep}}=(K_hK_w+1)C_{\mathrm{in}}
  +(C_{\mathrm{in}}+1)C_{\mathrm{out}}.
\end{equation}
A dense layer has $(N_{\mathrm{in}}+1)N_{\mathrm{out}}$ parameters.  For one
image, convolution MACs are counted as
$K_hK_wC_{\mathrm{in}}C_{\mathrm{out}}H_{\mathrm{out}}W_{\mathrm{out}}$
and dense MACs as $N_{\mathrm{in}}N_{\mathrm{out}}$; bias additions are not
counted.  Max pooling, ReLU, dropout, flatten, and GAP have no trainable
parameters.  To match the project profiler exactly, pooling and averaging
operations are also outside the reported MAC total.

each input channel and the pointwise $1\times1$ step mixes those channels.

GAP reduces the $128\times6\times6$ tensor to 128 channel means before the
dense stack, avoiding the $4{,}608\rightarrow128$ layer that dominates Model A.

The separable blocks contain $11{,}550$ parameters and the GAP-based dense
head contains $25{,}418$.  Model B is $63{,}032$ parameters below the
100,000-parameter limit.  Keeping a flattened head would leave a
$589{,}952$-parameter first dense layer, already over budget.  GAP lets us
keep the feature-channel widths; narrowing the channels would also reduce
the capacity of the feature extractor.

\subsubsection{Compute saving and profiler cross-check}

Table~\ref{tab:separable-mac-saving} isolates the convolution substitution at
identical input/output shapes.  Across the three stages it reduces convolution
MACs from $29{,}439{,}360$ to $3{,}699{,}980$, a $7.96\times$ reduction.  The
reduction increases as the channel count grows:
for $256\rightarrow256$, the exact per-location costs are
$9\times256\times256=589{,}824$ and
$9\times256+256\times256=67{,}840$, giving an $8.69\times$ reduction.

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering

  convolution at each common stage shape.}

    $32\rightarrow64$, $29\times29$ & $15{,}501{,}312$ & $1{,}964{,}576$ & $7.89\times$ \\
    $64\rightarrow128$, $12\times12$ & $10{,}616{,}832$ & $1{,}262{,}592$ & $8.41\times$ \\

Including the classifier heads, Model B uses $18.72\times$ fewer trainable
parameters (a $94.66\%$ reduction) and $8.06\times$ fewer MACs per image
(an $87.60\%$ reduction) than Model A.  Actual inference time also depends on
memory traffic, kernel support, and operations omitted from the MAC count.

The forward-hook profiler gives $692{,}106$ parameters and $30{,}038{,}016$
MACs for Model A, and $36{,}968$ parameters and $3{,}725{,}196$ MACs for
Model B.  Both totals agree with the hand derivations.
